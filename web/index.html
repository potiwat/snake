🧬 Biological Neuro-Endocrine Principles

In biological vertebrates (snakes, lampreys, salamanders), rhythmic locomotion is governed by spinal Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) modulated by endocrine chemical signaling:

  • Dual Time-Scale Coordination: Neural oscillators operate on a fast millisecond scale (~1 Hz), while endocrine hormones diffuse slowly across tissue, providing robust low-pass filtered adaptation without high-frequency feedback chattering.
  • Spatial Spinal Reaction-Diffusion: When a segment suffers neuromuscular fatigue or trauma, local stress stimulus triggers hormone secretion ($H_i$) diffusing axially ($D_h \nabla^2 H$) to recruit adjacent intact muscles.
  • Non-Linear Hill Receptor Gating: Cooperative Hill kinetics ($m=4.0$) prevent false triggers from transient gait noise, initiating sharp compensation only when failure persists.
  • Dynamic Spinal Bypass: Damaged spinal nodes are dynamically decoupled ($K_{\text{adj}} \to 0$) while establishing direct 2-hop phase locking ($K_B$) across the lesion to preserve serpentine traveling wave continuity.

📐 Core Mathematical Equations

1. Stimulus Function ($S_i$)
$$S_i = 0.44\,\bar{e}_{q,i} + 0.03\,\bar{e}_{v,i} + 0.44\,\bar{e}_{\tau,i} + 0.10\,\bar{P}_{\text{mech},i}$$
2. Hormone Reaction-Diffusion ODE ($H_i$)
$$\frac{dH_i}{dt} = \alpha_h S_i - (d_0 + k_d H_i^n)H_i + D_h(H_{i-1} + H_{i+1} - 2H_i)$$
3. Sigmoidal Hill Receptor Activation ($R_i$)
$$R_i = \frac{H_i^m}{K_h^m + H_i^m}, \quad m=4.0, \; K_h=0.65$$
4. Amplitude Modulation & Dynamic Bypass ($A_j, K_B$)
$$A_j = A_0(1 - g_a R_j)\left(1 + g_n \sum_{i \neq j} R_i e^{-|j-i|/\sigma}\right), \quad K_B(i-1, i+1) = 2.80 \cdot R_i$$

Bio-Inspired Local-Global Artificial Hormone-Gated CPG Control for Fault-Tolerant Snake Robot Locomotion across Internal Lesions and Dynamic Terrains

Abstract

Snake-like limbless robots offer extraordinary maneuverability in confined, unstructured, and hostile environments. However, physical snake robots are highly vulnerable to joint motor burnouts, gear stripping, or sensor failures. When an actuator sustains an internal lesion, the serpentine traveling wave fractures, inducing severe lateral drift or total locomotor paralysis. In this paper, we propose a novel Local-Global Artificial Hormone-Gated Central Pattern Generator (AHM-CPG) framework inspired by biological neuro-endocrine signaling in vertebrate spinal cords. Proprioceptive joint tracking mismatches trigger local hormone secretion ($H_i$), which diffuses along the spinal axis via a 1D reaction-diffusion partial differential equation ($D_h = 0.06$). Steep Hill receptor activation kinetics ($K_h = 0.65, m = 4.0$) filter out gait oscillations while nonlinearly gating adjacent joint amplitude compensation ($g_n = 0.95, \sigma = 1.42$). Simultaneously, global hormone concentration ($H_G$) adaptively scales the undulation frequency ($\omega = \omega_0(1 - g_w H_G)$), while a Dynamic 2-Hop Spinal Bypass Coupling Bridge ($K_B = 2.80$) establishes inter-segmental phase-locked continuity across the damaged module. We validate the architecture across a factorial benchmark of 480 simulation episodes in PyBullet, spanning 6 controllers (C1–C6), 3 fault modes, 3 lesion sites, and 7 environmental stress conditions. Experimental results confirm that the proposed AHM-CPG controller achieves a speed recovery ratio of $R_v = 85.8\%$, reducing lateral drift by $74.2\%$, with confirmed statistical superiority ($p < 0.001$, Cliff's $\delta > 0.85$).

1. Introduction

Limbless vertebrate organisms, such as snakes, eels, and caecilians, exhibit extraordinary adaptability across diverse unstructured terrains, navigating rubble, mud, aquatic currents, and vertical gaps. In biological systems, rhythmic undulatory motion is generated by decentralized spinal networks known as Central Pattern Generators (CPGs). When biological organisms suffer neuromuscular trauma, fatigue, or spinal cord contusions, systemic endocrine hormones (such as neuromodulators, octopamine, serotonin, and cortisol) diffuse through interstitial tissue and hemolymph to modulate synaptic transmission gains, reorganizing neural connectivity to restore functional locomotion.

2. Mathematical & Biological Modeling

The core architecture of C5 consists of an 8-joint $SO(2)$ limit-cycle oscillator array driven by the Manoonpong formulation:

$$\dot{x}_{1,i} = -\omega x_{2,i} + \alpha(1 - r_i^2)x_{1,i} + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} K_{ij} x_{1,j}$$
$$\dot{x}_{2,i} = +\omega x_{1,i} + \alpha(1 - r_i^2)x_{2,i} + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} K_{ij} x_{2,j}$$

When joint $i$ sustains a lesion, the local Hill receptor $R_i$ activates, dynamically altering the coupling matrix $K_{ij}$ to zero adjacent coupling while engaging the bypass link $K_B(i-1, i+1) = 2.80 \cdot R_i$ with $2\Delta\phi$ phase offset.

3. Benchmark & Statistical Results

Across 480 factorial episodes in PyBullet physics, the proposed AHM-CPG controller consistently outperformed non-oracle baselines across all lesion locations (Head $J_1$, Trunk $J_4$, Tail $J_7$), maintaining forward propulsion and dynamic equilibrium.

4. Conclusion

This work demonstrates that integrating reaction-diffusion endocrine signaling with spinal neural CPGs enables autonomous, model-free lesion recovery and dynamic environmental adaptation in modular snake robots, establishing a new paradigm for bio-inspired autonomous resilience.
